Consultants sell expertise. But expertise is invisible until it's communicated well. Your website is where that happens. Here's what a consultant's site should include to turn visitors into clients.

## A positioning statement

One sentence that says who you help and what problem you solve. "I help B2B SaaS companies reduce churn" beats "I'm a business consultant" every time. Specificity is what makes you memorable.

## Your services

Not a menu of twenty things. Three to five clearly defined offerings. Each one should answer: what is it, who is it for, and what outcome does it produce?

## Your process

Consulting clients want to know what working with you actually looks like. Discovery call, assessment, recommendations, implementation support — even a simple four-step outline removes uncertainty and increases inquiries.

## Case studies or results

One or two short case studies do more than a page of testimonials. Show the problem, what you did, and what changed. Numbers help if you have them.

## Who you work with (and who you don't)

Naming your ideal client — industry, size, stage — attracts the right people and quietly filters out the wrong ones. This is one of the highest-leverage things a consultant's website can do.

## A clear next step

Book a call. Request a proposal. Send an inquiry. Whatever your next step is, make it obvious and easy to take.

Consultants often assume their reputation does the selling. It does — until someone looks you up and finds nothing. A clean, credible website closes that gap.
