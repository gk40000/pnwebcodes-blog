Contractors and tradespeople rarely need a fancy website. They need one that does three simple things: show their work, name their service area, and make it easy to call. Here's what to include.

## Photos of finished work

This is the single most important element. Before-and-after photos, finished projects, detail shots — real images build more trust than any amount of copy. Organize by project type so visitors can find what's relevant to them.

## Your services

List what you actually do: plumbing, electrical, roofing, remodeling, painting, landscaping. Be specific about the work you take on and, if relevant, the work you don't.

## Your service area

Name the towns, neighborhoods, or counties you cover. This helps you rank in local searches and pre-qualifies inquiries.

## Credentials and licenses

License numbers, insurance, certifications, years in business. In trades, these are trust signals that matter to homeowners making decisions about their property.

## Testimonials

Short quotes from past clients, ideally with first names, towns, and project types. Local testimonials are especially powerful.

## A phone number people can find instantly

Put your phone number in the header, and make it clickable on mobile. Most contractor inquiries are phone calls. Don't make people hunt for the number.

## A simple quote or contact form

For people who prefer not to call, a short form that asks for name, project type, location, and contact info works well. Keep it minimal.

A contractor's website should be practical, honest, and easy to use. Skip the trends — focus on the work, the area, and the phone number.
