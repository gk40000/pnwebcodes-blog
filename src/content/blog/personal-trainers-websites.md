Personal training is a local, relationship-driven business. Your website should make it easy for potential clients to understand what you offer, where you train, and how to get started. Here's what to include.

## Your training philosophy

Are you strength-focused? Functional? Rehabilitation-oriented? Sports performance? A short, clear statement of how you train helps the right clients find you and self-select in.

## Services and formats

One-on-one sessions, small group training, online coaching, nutrition guidance, program design. List each offering with a short description and a clear price or pricing range.

## Your location or training options

If you train in a gym, name it. If you train clients at home, say so. If you offer online coaching, highlight it. Location is one of the first things clients check.

## Before-and-after results

Results matter in this business. Even a few client transformations, presented honestly, do more than any headline. Make sure you have permission before posting.

## Credentials

NASM, ACE, NSCA, CSCS, or specialty certifications. List them — but more importantly, briefly explain what they mean if clients might not know.

## Scheduling

A booking link, a trial session offer, or a simple contact form. Personal training has a low-commitment entry point: make it easy for someone to try a session before committing to a package.

## Your photo and bio

Clients want to know who they'll be spending hours with. A real photo and a short, personal bio build trust before the first session.

A personal trainer's website should feel energetic but not intimidating — inviting, professional, and easy to act on.
