Accounting and bookkeeping are built on trust, precision, and reliability. Your website should communicate all three — calmly and clearly. Here's what to include.

## Your services, precisely defined

Tax preparation, bookkeeping, payroll, CFO services, audit support — list what you actually do. Vague phrases like "financial services" tell clients nothing and attract the wrong inquiries.

## Who you serve

Sole traders? Small businesses? Nonprofits? High-net-worth individuals? Naming your ideal client makes it easier for the right people to self-identify and reach out.

## Your credentials

CPA, EA, CMA, QuickBooks ProAdvisor — list them prominently and explain them briefly. In financial services, credentials are trust signals.

## Your process

How does a new client engagement work? Discovery call, document collection, onboarding, ongoing service? A short section removes uncertainty and reduces back-and-forth emails.

## Pricing structure

You don't have to list exact fees, but you should indicate whether you charge hourly, monthly, or by project. Accounting clients hate surprises — transparency here wins business.

## Deadlines and important dates

A simple calendar of tax deadlines or filing dates is genuinely useful and keeps clients returning to your site.

## A secure way to get in touch

A contact form and, ideally, a client portal link if you use one. Security and confidentiality should be visible, not assumed.

Accountants who invest in a clean, professional website attract better clients — and spend less time explaining what they do.
