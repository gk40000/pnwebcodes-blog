Real estate is a trust business. Buyers and sellers are making one of the largest financial decisions of their lives, and they need to feel confident in the person guiding them. A website is where that confidence gets built — before the first phone call.

Here's what a real estate professional's site should include.

## Your service areas

Be specific. "Serving North Dallas and surrounding suburbs" is far more useful than "serving the metro area." Local specificity builds credibility and helps you rank in searches for your neighborhoods.

## Buyers and sellers — separate pages

Buyers and sellers have different concerns. Buyers want to know about listings, neighborhoods, and the buying process. Sellers want to know about pricing, marketing, and timelines. Address each audience on its own page.

## Featured listings or recent sales

A rotating selection of current listings or recent closings shows that you're active and effective. Even a simple grid of photos with addresses and status works well.

## Neighborhood guides

Short guides to the areas you serve — schools, amenities, commute times, price ranges — are gold for buyers who are new to the area. They also rank well in search.

## Testimonials

Real estate is referral-driven. Testimonials from past clients reassure new ones that you deliver on your promises.

## A simple way to reach you

Phone, email, and a contact form. Buyers and sellers often reach out at odd hours. Make it easy for them to leave a message whenever they're ready.

Real estate websites don't need to be flashy. They need to be local, specific, and easy to navigate — because the people visiting them are making decisions that matter.
