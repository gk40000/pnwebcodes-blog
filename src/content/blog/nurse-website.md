Nursing is one of the most trusted professions in the world. But trust doesn't automatically travel online. If you're a nurse building a private practice, offering consulting, or launching a side business, you need a website that reflects the same professionalism your patients already associate with your work.

Here's what a nurse's professional website should include.

## Your credentials, clearly stated

RN, BSN, MSN, NP, CRNA — whatever your letters are, put them near your name and explain what they mean. Not every visitor knows the difference between an RN and an NP. A short, plain sentence removes the guesswork.

## Your specialty

Nursing is broad. Are you an ICU nurse? A pediatric nurse? A nurse educator? A telehealth consultant? Naming your focus helps the right people find you and helps the wrong ones move on quickly — which is good for everyone.

## Services you offer

If you're offering patient advocacy, health coaching, consulting for families, or clinical education, describe each service in one clear paragraph. Don't make people guess.

## Your approach

A short section about how you work — how you communicate, what a typical engagement looks like, what you expect from clients — reduces mismatches and builds confidence.

## A clear way to reach you

Phone, email, and a contact form. If you offer telehealth or consulting, a booking link makes the next step obvious.

## Trust signals

A professional photo. Testimonials if you have them. A license number if relevant. A short bio that reads like a person, not a résumé.

Nurses bring extraordinary credibility into any business they build. A clean, professional website simply makes sure that credibility arrives before they do.
